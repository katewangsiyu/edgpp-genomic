\subsection{$\hat\sigma$ class ablation: GBM vs.\ MLP vs.\ Random Forest}
\label{app:tables:sigma_ablation}

T5.1's dimension-free $O(n^{-1/2})$ rate (Theorem~\ref{thm:t5oracle})
builds on \ref{ass:ASL}: $\shat$ is a 1-D Lipschitz summary with
$L_F < \infty$. The headline LCLS estimate $\hat L_F^{\mathrm{GBM}} = 2.97 / 4.51$
(Mendelian / Complex) was computed on the single GBM-based $\shat$ used
throughout the paper. Reviewers reasonably ask whether the dimension-free
property depends on a smoothness property of the GBM architecture rather
than a property of the conformal construction.

To stress-test this, we re-train two alternative $\shat$ heads on identical
features (CADD+GPN-MSA+Borzoi, $d \approx 8500$), identical chrom-LOO
protocol, and identical Day~10 GBM aggregator $\phat$:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{MLP}: 3-layer $64\text{-}32\text{-}1$ with softplus output and
AdamW training on the absolute-residual target ($80$ epochs, lr $5{\cdot}10^{-3}$,
weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch $256$, dropout $0.1$).
\item \textbf{Random Forest}: $50$-tree ensemble with
\texttt{max\_features="sqrt"} (a non-smooth, piecewise-constant $\shat$ used
as the worst-case stress test for \ref{ass:ASL}).
\end{itemize}
Both heads share the same supervised target $|r_i| = |y_i - \phat^{(-C_i)}(x_i)|$
as the GBM head. Per-head LCLS $\hat L_F$ (regression slope of pairwise KS
on $\sigma$-gap), HCCP single-seed worst-cell $\shat$-bin gap at the
recommended $K_{\mathrm{eval}}$, and Spearman$(\shat, |r|)$ are reported below.

\paragraph{Reading the ablation.}
\textbf{On Complex} ($n = 11{,}400$, per-cell minority $\approx 200$): all three
$\shat$ classes give worst-cell gap $\in [0.011, 0.015]$ and minority-coverage
within $\pm 0.005$ of target. $\hat L_F$ varies by less than $2\times$ across
classes ($2.59$ to $4.67$). The dimension-free claim of T5.1 is empirically
robust to $\shat$ architecture once $n$ is large enough to keep per-cell
minority above the variance-stable floor.

\textbf{On Mendelian} ($n = 3{,}380$, per-cell minority $\in [3, 316]$): the
worst-cell gaps span three orders of magnitude ($0.017, 0.047, 0.900$). Two
diagnostics resolve this:
\begin{itemize}
\item The MLP's nominal $\hat L_F = 74$ is an artifact of $\shat$-collapse:
the 64-channel projection cannot capture the $d \approx 8500$ feature space at
$n = 3{,}380$, and the MLP's output collapses to near-zero ($\shat$ q05/q95 $=
4{\cdot}10^{-5} / 3{\cdot}10^{-5}$, Spearman $= 0.125$). The pairwise
$\sigma$-gaps in the LCLS estimator collapse to numerical floor, inflating the
KS/gap ratio. Operationally HCCP degenerates to class-conditional split CP via
the small-cell pooled fallback (Alg.~\ref{alg:hccp}, line 6); the gap of
$0.017$ is a class-cond split CP property, not an HCCP property.
\item The Random Forest's nominal worst-cell gap of $0.900$ is driven by a
single minority cell ($y = 1$, $b = 0$, $n = 5$, coverage $= 0/5$): RF's
heavy-tailed $\shat$ distribution puts $5$ minority variants in the lowest
$\shat$-bin where the per-chrom calibration quantile is misspecified. The
\emph{second}-worst cell of Mendelian/RF has gap $0.013$. With a stricter
$\min$-cell threshold ($\geq 10$) the worst-cell gap drops to $0.013$,
comparable to GBM. Sample-size noise drives the $0.900$ figure, not a
violation of \ref{ass:ASL}.
\end{itemize}

The Mendelian discrepancies are consistent with Eq.~\eqref{eq:gap_decomp}:
the variance term $K / (\pi_{\min} n)$ dominates at $n = 3{,}380$, $K = 3$,
$\pi_{\min} = 0.10$, putting the empirical worst-cell gap floor at
$\sim K / (\pi_{\min} n) \approx 0.009$ even before any $\shat$-related bias,
plus a small-cell binomial tail that adds variant-count outliers. T5.1's
upper bound $G(K^\star) \leq 2 \sqrt{L_F R / (\pi_{\min} n)}$ remains valid for
all three $\shat$ classes, but at small $n$ the empirical gap is dominated by
the small-cell variance regime that $\shat$ choice cannot cure --- precisely
the regime boundary already documented in \S\ref{sec:experiments:h2h} (R1) and
Tab.~\ref{tab:keval_sensitivity}. \textbf{Take-away: the dimension-free
property of T5.1 is architecture-agnostic on Complex (where the rate regime
binds); on Mendelian, the small-cell variance regime binds, and we recommend
GBM as the operational $\shat$ class because it gives the best Spearman fit
(less small-cell noise) at finite $L_F$.}
